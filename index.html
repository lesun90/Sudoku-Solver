    A Sudoku solver that thinks like you do

    Generate

    Fresh puzzles in four levels. Easy and Medium fall to singles alone, Hard needs locked candidates or pairs, and Expert needs real search. Every puzzle has exactly one solution.

    Play

    Pencil marks, automatic candidates, undo, error checking and hints that name the technique, such as a naked or hidden single, rather than just showing the answer.

    Create

    Type or paste any Sudoku from a newspaper, a book or a friend. The editor shows conflicts as you type, then reports whether the puzzle has no solution, one solution or many, and rates its difficulty.

    Solve

    The auto-solver propagates pencil-mark logic and falls back to depth-first search with backtracking. It fills the board step by step and reports nodes, guesses and techniques used.

    Units, peers & candidates

    A unit is a row, a column or a 3×3 box. Each digit 1–9 appears exactly once in every unit.

    The cells that share a unit with a cell are its peers. Every cell has 20 of them, shaded in the diagram.

    Candidates, also called pencil marks, are the small digits that could still go in an empty cell: every digit not already used by one of its peers. Every technique below works by crossing candidates out.

    To write your own marks, press N or hold ⇧ while typing a digit. Marks fills them all in for you.

    Naked single

    A cell has only one candidate left. Every other digit is already used in its row, column or box, so that last digit must go there.

    In the diagram, the centre cell can only be 4.

    This is the easiest technique to spot once pencil marks are filled in: look for a cell with a single small digit.

    Hidden single

    Within one row, column or box, a digit fits in only one cell. The digit must go there, even if that cell still has other candidates. That is why the single is "hidden".

    In this row, 6 appears among the candidates of only one cell, so that cell is 6.

    Scan one unit at a time and ask, for each missing digit, "where can it go?"

    Locked candidates: pointing

    Inside a box, every candidate for a digit sits in a single row or column. The box must put that digit somewhere on that line, so the rest of the line cannot have it.

    In the left box, 4 can only go in the top row. Cross 4 out of the rest of that row (red).

    Locked candidates: claiming

    The reverse of pointing. Inside a row or column, every candidate for a digit sits in a single box. The line claims that digit for itself, so the rest of that box cannot have it.

    In the middle row, 7 can only go in the right box. Cross 7 out of that box's other rows.

    Naked pair

    Two cells in the same unit have exactly the same two candidates. Between them, they will use both digits, so no other cell in that unit can have either one.

    The two {2, 8} cells take 2 and 8, so cross both digits out of the rest of the row.

    Guessing (search & backtracking)

    When no technique makes progress, the solver guesses. It picks the cell with the fewest candidates (usually two) and tries one of them. Then it applies all the techniques again.

    If that leads to a contradiction (a cell with no candidates left, or a digit with nowhere to go), the guess was wrong. The solver backtracks to the guess and tries the next candidate.

    On the board, teal cells were placed after a guess. Hints never guess. When no single is available, a hint reveals the cell the solver would branch on.

    Difficulty levels

    • EasyNaked and hidden singles are enough. Many givens (36–40).
    • MediumSingles only, but fewer givens (28–32), so they are harder to spot.
    • HardNeeds locked candidates or naked pairs. No guessing.
    • ExpertNone of the techniques here can finish it. The solver has to guess.

    Every generated puzzle has exactly one solution. In Create mode, puzzles you enter are checked and rated the same way.

    Solver report terms

    Givens / clues
    The digits the puzzle starts with.
    Search nodes
    Positions the solver examined. 1 means logic alone solved it.
    Guesses
    Candidates the solver tried during search.
    Dead ends
    Guesses that led to a contradiction and were undone.
    Search depth
    The most guesses stacked on top of each other at once.
    Eliminations
    How often pointing, claiming or naked pairs removed candidates.
    Unique solution
    Exactly one valid completion exists. A proper Sudoku always has one.
